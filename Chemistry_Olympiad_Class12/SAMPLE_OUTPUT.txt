QUESTION 1

Topic: Aldehydes & Ketones
Difficulty: Medium
Marks: 4 | Time: 3-4 minutes

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Question:
A compound (C₅H₁₀O) gives a positive 2,4-DNP test but does not undergo
iodoform reaction. It shows two doublets in its ¹H-NMR spectrum. The compound
also does not decolorize bromine water. What is the most likely structure?

A) CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-CH₂-CHO
B) CH₃-CH₂-CO-CH₂-CH₃
C) (CH₃)₂CH-CH₂-CHO
D) (CH₃)₂CH-CO-CH₃

Correct Answer: C

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Solution:
Let's analyze each clue systematically:

Step 1: Positive 2,4-DNP test
   → Confirms presence of carbonyl group (aldehyde or ketone)

Step 2: Negative iodoform test
   → Rules out CH₃-CO- or CH₃-CH(OH)- groups
   → This eliminates options B and D as they contain CH₃-CO-

Step 3: Two doublets in ¹H-NMR
   → Suggests two different protons in similar but not identical environments
   → Each coupled to one neighboring proton
   → Pattern: -CH(CH₃)₂ group next to -CHO

Step 4: Does not decolorize bromine water
   → No C=C double bond present
   → This is a saturated aldehyde

Step 5: Molecular formula C₅H₁₀O
   → Degree of unsaturation = 1 (accounted by C=O)

Combining all clues:
The compound must be (CH₃)₂CH-CH₂-CHO [3-methylbutanal]

This structure:
✓ Has aldehyde group (positive 2,4-DNP)
✓ No CH₃-CO- group (negative iodoform)
✓ Shows two doublets: one for (CH₃)₂CH- and one for -CHO
✓ No C=C bond (doesn't decolorize Br₂ water)

Answer: C

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Concepts Tested:
• Carbonyl group tests (2,4-DNP, iodoform)
• NMR spectroscopy interpretation
• Structure determination from spectral data
• Aldehyde vs ketone identification

Common Mistake:
Students often select option D thinking that any methyl ketone gives iodoform
test. However, they miss that option D has CH₃-CO- which WOULD give positive
iodoform test, contradicting the given data.

Olympiad Insight:
This question tests the ability to combine multiple pieces of spectroscopic
and chemical evidence to arrive at a unique structure. Olympiad-level chemistry
requires systematic elimination and understanding why each test gives specific
results, not just memorizing the tests themselves.

QUESTION 2

Topic: Chemical Kinetics
Difficulty: Hard
Marks: 4 | Time: 4-5 minutes

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Question:
For a reaction: 2A + B → Products, the following data was obtained:

Experiment | [A]₀ (M) | [B]₀ (M) | Initial rate (M/s)
-----------|----------|----------|-------------------
    1      |   0.1    |   0.1    |     2.0 × 10⁻³
    2      |   0.2    |   0.1    |     8.0 × 10⁻³
    3      |   0.1    |   0.2    |     4.0 × 10⁻³

If the initial concentration of both A and B is 0.3 M, what will be the
initial rate of reaction?

A) 5.4 × 10⁻² M/s
B) 2.7 × 10⁻² M/s
C) 1.08 × 10⁻¹ M/s
D) 3.6 × 10⁻² M/s

Correct Answer: A

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Solution:

Step 1: Determine order with respect to A
Compare Experiments 1 and 2 ([B] constant):
   When [A] doubles: 0.1 → 0.2
   Rate changes: 2.0×10⁻³ → 8.0×10⁻³
   Rate multiplies by 4

   Rate₂/Rate₁ = ([A]₂/[A]₁)ⁿ
   4 = (2)ⁿ
   n = 2

   Order with respect to A = 2

Step 2: Determine order with respect to B
Compare Experiments 1 and 3 ([A] constant):
   When [B] doubles: 0.1 → 0.2
   Rate changes: 2.0×10⁻³ → 4.0×10⁻³
   Rate multiplies by 2

   Rate₃/Rate₁ = ([B]₃/[B]₁)ᵐ
   2 = (2)ᵐ
   m = 1

   Order with respect to B = 1

Step 3: Write rate law
   Rate = k[A]²[B]

Step 4: Calculate rate constant k
Using Experiment 1:
   2.0×10⁻³ = k(0.1)²(0.1)
   2.0×10⁻³ = k(0.001)
   k = 2.0 M⁻²s⁻¹

Step 5: Calculate rate when [A]₀ = [B]₀ = 0.3 M
   Rate = k[A]²[B]
   Rate = 2.0 × (0.3)² × (0.3)
   Rate = 2.0 × 0.09 × 0.3
   Rate = 2.0 × 0.027
   Rate = 0.054 M/s = 5.4 × 10⁻² M/s

Answer: A

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Concepts Tested:
• Determination of order from experimental data
• Method of initial rates
• Rate law expression
• Calculation of rate constant
• Units of rate constant

Common Mistake:
Many students assume the order matches the stoichiometric coefficients (2 for A,
1 for B from the balanced equation). While this happens to be correct here, it's
a coincidence! Order must ALWAYS be determined experimentally, not from the
balanced equation. Some students also make calculation errors when finding k or
the final rate.

Olympiad Insight:
This question emphasizes that reaction order is an experimental quantity, not
a theoretical one. The order can only be determined from experimental data.
Advanced kinetics often involves complex rate laws where orders are fractional
or don't match coefficients, testing deeper understanding of reaction mechanisms.
